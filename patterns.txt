TWO POINTERS

The Two-Pointer technique is fundamentally a way to reduce O(N²) nested loops down to O(N) by leveraging a known property of the data (like sorting, sequence, or contiguous memory).

3. THE THREE MAJOR IDENTIFICATION TRIGGERS

"Sorted" + "Pair/Target"

The Clue: You need to find two numbers that sum to X, or find the closest pair. The array is sorted.

The Approach: Opposite-direction. Start at 0 and N-1. Move inward based on whether the sum is too big or too small.

"In-Place" + "Modify/Filter/Partition"

The Clue: You need to remove duplicates, move zeros to the end, or segregate evens/odds using exactly O(1) extra space.

The Approach: Same-direction (Slow/Fast). One pointer explores, the other pointer marks the boundary of the "clean" data.

"Symmetry" or "Subsequence"

The Clue: You are validating a palindrome, reversing data, or checking if sequence A exists chronologically within sequence B.

The Approach: Opposite-direction for symmetry/reversals. Two-sequence Same-direction for subsequences.

4. THE DISGUISE (ADVANCED RECOGNITION)
Sometimes the "sorted" property means the extremes of the data live at the ends. For example, in an array containing both negative and positive numbers (e.g., [-5, -2, 0, 1, 4]), the largest absolute values (and therefore the largest squares: 25 and 16) are located at the far left and far right. If you need to process them by size, opposite-direction pointers can evaluate both ends and pick the largest one.
